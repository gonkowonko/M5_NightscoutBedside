M5_NightscoutBedside

A calm bedside clock with your Nightscout glucose, for the original M5Stack Basic. Version {{VERSION}}.

Not a medical device

This is a hobby project. Readings can be delayed, missing or wrong. Never use it to make treatment decisions, and don't rely on it in place of your CGM app's alerts. It has no sound alarms.

You'll need

  • An original M5Stack Basic Core. The BTC Standing Base is optional; it adds a room temperature sensor.
  • Chrome or Edge on a computer. Browser flashing doesn't work in Safari, Firefox or on phones.
  • A USB-A to USB-C data cable. USB-C to USB-C cables don't work with the original Basic. On a USB-C-only Mac, use a USB-A adapter.
  • A microSD card (FAT32) for the settings file.

1 · Prepare the SD card

  1. Download the example M5NS.INI.
  2. Open it in a text editor and fill in your Nightscout address and token, your Wi-Fi name and password, and show_mgdl (0 = mmol/L, 1 = mg/dL).
  3. Copy it to the root of the card and put the card in the M5Stack.

Coming from M5_NightscoutMon? Keep your existing card. It works as it is.

2 · Install

Installing replaces the firmware on the M5Stack. To keep a copy of what's on it now, see backing up first.

Your browser can't flash devices. Use Chrome or Edge on a computer. Browser flashing needs a secure (https) page.

Plug the M5Stack in, press the button and pick the port that appears, usually CP2104 or usbserial. If no port shows up, try another cable or install the CP210x driver.

3 · After installing

  • The clock connects to Wi-Fi, sets the time and shows your first reading in about 20 seconds.
  • Open http://m5clock.local on your phone to change settings. Button B shows the IP address if that name doesn't work.
  • The clock checks for new versions every 2 days. When one's available, press Install on the settings page. No cable needed.
  • Buttons: A brightness, B status and log pages, C refresh now.
